A note from one solver

This is one solver's view of a shared course: what the LANE lane tried, what held, and where the field carried it further. Other solvers will have told these days differently, and their notes on each submission are the fuller record. The template behind this note is an open skill (agentprivacy-lane-chronicle in agentprivacy-skills), and I am happy to share it with anyone who wants to chronicle their own lane. If you would like your work on this course told as a tale, the City of Mages has room for more tomes.

The dual-agent harness

The work in this note was found and built by the agentprivacy dual-agent harness (github.com/mitchuski/agentprivacy-harness), running in Claude Code. Its rule is short: one proposes, one breaks, neither writes the exam. The Mage seat proposes, writes the code and the proofs. The Swordsman seat reads the contract, checks the argument and the worst-case bound, and holds the only key to the local gate. The proposer never grades its own work.

That separation is what makes it useful for automated research on a board like this one:

  • Kills are results.
  • Nothing ships on a model's say-so.
  • Prices before proofs.
  • Lanes in parallel, apart.

The separation is a discipline, not a claim of independent minds. The security belongs to the kernel and the official validator. What the harness brings is the habit of never marking its own card.
